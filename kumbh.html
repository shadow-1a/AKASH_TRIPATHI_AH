What is the Kumbh Mela?

The Kumbh Mela is a major Hindu pilgrimage and festival. Millions of devotees gather to bathe in sacred rivers, believing that a dip at the right time washes away sins and moves the soul toward liberation (moksha).

It rotates among four holy places in India: Prayagraj, Haridwar, Ujjain and Nashik. The word "Kumbh" means a pot or pitcher, and "Mela" means a fair or gathering.

In 2017, UNESCO added the Kumbh Mela to its list of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

Where Did the Kumbh Mela Originate?

The legend of Samudra Manthan

According to Hindu mythology, the gods (Devas) and demons (Asuras) once churned the cosmic ocean to obtain amrit, the nectar of immortality. When the golden pot (kumbh) of nectar emerged, a fierce struggle began over it.

While the pot was being carried across the sky, drops of nectar fell at four places on Earth: Prayagraj, Haridwar, Ujjain and Nashik. These places became the sites of the Kumbh Mela, and the rivers there are believed to carry the sacred power of the nectar.

This is a tradition and belief handed down through the Puranas, not a historical record. Historians note that large river-bathing gatherings at these places have very old roots.

Types of Kumbh at Prayagraj

Maha Kumbh

Held at Prayagraj once every 12 years. The largest and most sacred.

Purna Kumbh

The full Kumbh, held every 12 years at each of the four cities in turn.

Ardh Kumbh

The "half" Kumbh, held every 6 years at Prayagraj and Haridwar.

Magh Mela

An annual fair on the Sangam banks in the month of Magh (Jan–Feb).

Bathing dates are set by astrological positions of Jupiter, the Sun and the Moon. Always check the official dates before planning a trip.

History of the Kumbh Mela

Ancient roots

Gatherings at river confluences are very old in Indian tradition. The Puranas praise bathing at Prayaga, the old name of Prayagraj, as one of the holiest acts.

7th century CE

The Chinese pilgrim Xuanzang (Hiuen Tsang) visited the region during the reign of King Harsha and described a great gathering at Prayaga. Many scholars point to this account as an early description of a mela here.

Rise of the Akharas

Orders of sadhus called Akharas, including the Naga sadhus, became central to the Kumbh. They lead the grand ceremonial processions and first baths.

Medieval to colonial times

The Mela continued through Mughal and British rule, and the administration of the fair slowly became more organised as the crowds grew.

After Independence

The government began managing the Mela on a large scale, with temporary cities, bridges, roads, sanitation, medical camps and police arrangements.

2017

UNESCO inscribed the Kumbh Mela on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

2019 and 2025

Prayagraj hosted the Ardh Kumbh in 2019 and the Maha Kumbh in early 2025. Check official sources for the next dates.

How Prayagraj Originated

Prayagraj is one of India's oldest living cities. Its ancient name is Prayaga, meaning "place of sacrifice". Hindu tradition says Lord Brahma performed a great yajna (sacrifice) here, which is why it is called Tirtharaj, the "king of pilgrimage sites".

The city grew on the banks of the Ganga and Yamuna, whose meeting point, the Sangam, has been a pilgrimage place for thousands of years. It is mentioned in ancient texts, and the old city of Kaushambi nearby was an important centre in early times.

Timeline of Prayagraj

Ancient period

Known as Prayaga, a major holy place in the Puranas and epics.

7th century CE

Visited by Xuanzang, who described it as a prosperous and sacred place.

1583 to 1584: Mughal era

Emperor Akbar built the great fort at the Sangam, and the city came to be called Illahabas, later Allahabad.

18th and 19th centuries

The city passed to the East India Company and then the British Crown. It was a centre of the 1857 uprising.

1860s to 1880s

The Allahabad High Court was established (1866) and the University of Allahabad was founded (1887), making the city an education and legal hub.

Freedom movement

Allahabad was a key centre of the national movement. It was home to the Nehru family at Anand Bhawan and hosted several Indian National Congress sessions.

1931

Revolutionary Chandrashekhar Azad died at Alfred Park, now called Chandrashekhar Azad Park.

2018

The city was officially renamed Prayagraj.

  • Holy dip (snan) at the Sangam

  • Evening Ganga aarti
  • Kalpvas: devotees living simply by the river

  • Carry ID and keep valuables safe

  • Follow official crowd and bathing rules
